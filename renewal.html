The plan · Luke Nathan Hayes

Build for the next useful generation

The long-lived local asset is the capability around the rack, not a promise that today’s hardware stays frontier forever.

Three connected lifecycles

Buildings, site access, electrical routes and heat connections have one lifecycle. UPS, cooling equipment, controls and networking have another. Processors, memory, storage and rack-scale computing have their own replacement and upgrade decisions. The design records these separately rather than applying one depreciation or replacement assumption to everything.

A documented upgrade envelope

At installation, record the available electrical capacity, cooling capability, floor loading, space, handling route, fibre and service access. Identify what can be expanded and what requires further site work. Reserve practical expansion paths where justified without buying every imagined future requirement on day one.

Upgrade when the work justifies it

Review capability against workloads, demand, operating cost, efficiency, reliability, security and support. A new generation may justify a replacement, an additional node, a changed service mix or no immediate purchase. Performance comparisons belong to the actual work being delivered, not only headline processor specifications.

Redeploy the useful equipment

Previous-generation equipment can remain in service, move to another suitable workload or location, be sold through an agreed secondary-market route, or enter responsible recycling. A redeployment decision includes its remaining support, power and cooling needs, transport, preparation, data handling and the receiving operator’s responsibilities.

Renewal without restarting extraction

Build technology-renewal funding into the fleet model and the co-operative’s acquisition case. Separate the repayment of original finance from the cost of maintaining useful future capability. Each upgrade has an agreed scope, cost and ownership arrangement; it does not silently reinstate a permanent investor claim over the entire community network.

Keep knowledge current with the fleet

Record each hardware and software release in the site twin, refresh the relevant training material and rehearse changed maintenance and recovery tasks. The next installation benefits from measured experience with earlier generations while retaining a clear account of what has changed.
